From built to paid, in eight weeks

The app is finished enough to put in front of real families. What stands between here and taking money is hosting, row-level security, a survey that asks the right things, and Paddle's verification queue — which is the slowest item and the one to start first. Work down the page; nothing in a phase should start before its gate is clear.

0 of 0 done

Four things to decide before week 1

Each one changes what gets built, so settle them on paper first.

  1. Trial with or without a card. Recommendation: without. A family product lives or dies on the first evening; asking for a card before they've seen a single streak will cost more sign-ups than it saves you in chasing. It means the trial lives in your own database rather than Paddle's.
  2. Whether to sell Pro at launch. The site promises weekly email reports, reward-waiting emails and a second parent login. None of that is built. Either build it or take Pro off the page until it exists.
  3. Which prices to test. The site currently shows $3.99 and $6.99 a month. Treat those as a hypothesis the day‑14 survey either confirms or replaces.
  4. Who the eight families are. Name them now. Recruiting during the pilot is how pilots drift for a month.

Settled 21 Sep: the product is Unprompted, published by MVB, and MVB is the highest mark a child can earn in the app.

Week 1gate

Before another family's data exists

Everything here is a safety or plumbing job. None of it is visible to a parent, and all of it is much harder to fix once other people's children are in the database.

Hard stop No invitations go out until row-level security is on and verified. The publishable Supabase key ships inside the page and sits in a GitHub repo — RLS is the only thing separating one family's data from another's.

Do the Paddle sign-up this week too — verification is the long pole, and it runs while you pilot.

Weeks 2–4pilot

Eight families, watched closely

At this size you do not need analytics. You need to sit with people while they set it up and write down every place they pause. Eight is enough to find the problems and few enough to answer personally.

Needs Week 1 complete. Each family gets a real account on the live address — not the demo.
Days 3 & 14survey

Two short surveys, not one long one

The day‑3 survey is about setup, while it is still fresh. The day‑14 survey is about value and price, once there is something to judge. Keep both under five minutes or you will get four replies.

Day 3 — did setup work?

5 questions · send the evening of day 3

  1. How long did setting it up take? Under 10 min · 10–20 · 20–40 · I gave up
  2. What was confusing, if anything? Free text. This is the one that earns its keep.
  3. Which did you set up? Parent PIN · deadlines · timetable · rewards · none yet
  4. Did your child pick their own character and theme? Yes / no — a proxy for whether they took ownership.
  5. Was anything missing that you expected? Free text.

Day 14 — is it worth money?

8 questions · send the morning after the trial ends

  1. On how many of the 14 days did your child use it?
  2. Did you give fewer reminders? Many fewer · a few fewer · about the same · more
  3. What did your child care about most? Points · rewards · themes · their character's marks · streaks
  4. What nearly made you stop using it? Free text.
  5. At what monthly price would it be too cheap to trust, a bargain, getting expensive, and too expensive? Four prices, four answers. Do not ask "would you pay $3.99" — people say yes to be kind.
  6. How many children would you use it for?
  7. Yearly at two months free, or monthly?
  8. Would you recommend it to another parent? 0–10.

Ask question 5 before you finalise the price on the site — not after.

Weeks 2–6billing

Paddle, started early and finished late

Start this in week 2 alongside the pilot, because verification is out of your hands and can take a fortnight. Everything after verification is a day's work — the checkout code on the site is written and tested already.

Long pole Seller verification needs a live website, your business or personal details and a payout bank account. Submit it the same week the site goes up.
Weeks 4–6build

Make the trial real

Today the 15 days are a promise on a page: nothing records when a trial started, counts the days or stops anyone at the end. This is the work that makes the business model exist.

Order Build this after the pilot has told you what to change, and before anyone is asked to pay.
Weeks 7–8launch

Open the doors, slowly

A soft launch to parents who already know you, then wider. The demo account is the marketing — it lets a parent see a month of real-looking progress before they give you anything.

Needs A pilot family has taken a subscription end to end, and an expired trial has been seen with your own eyes.

What could go wrong

Ordered by what would cost the most to recover from, not by how likely they are.

Severe
One family reads another's data

The publishable key is in the page and in a public repo by design; that is normal, and harmless only while RLS is on. Verify it with two accounts before any invitation goes out, and again after every schema change.

Severe
Charging for Pro features that do not exist

The site sells weekly email reports, reward-waiting emails and a second parent login. None are built. Build them or remove them from the page before the first Pro subscription is taken.

Watch
Paddle verification lands after you are ready to sell

Nothing you can do to speed it up, which is exactly why it starts in week 2 rather than week 6.

Watch
iPhone families never install the app

Apple allows it only from Safari, via Share then Add to Home Screen. The app explains this now, but expect to walk at least one pilot parent through it, and count how many manage it alone.

Watch
The pilot turns into a rebuild

Eight engaged parents will generate more ideas than you can ship. Batch the changes weekly and keep a list of everything you are deliberately not doing yet.
